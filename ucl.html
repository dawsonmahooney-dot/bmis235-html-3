UCL Tears and Sprains

The ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) runs along the inside of the elbow and holds the upper and lower arm bones together when you throw. Repeated hard throwing can stretch it (a sprain) or partly or fully tear it.

Signs include pain on the inner elbow when throwing, losing velocity or control, and sometimes feeling a "pop." Sprains and partial tears are often treated with rest and rehab. A full tear in a pitcher is often repaired with UCL reconstruction, known as Tommy John surgery, which can take about a year or more to come back from.

Pitch count limits help prevent UCL injuries. See the MLB Pitch Smart guidelines.
